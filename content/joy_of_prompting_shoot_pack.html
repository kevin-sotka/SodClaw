01 — THE BUY

Skip the Zoom. Buy a wireless lav and an overhead arm.

You asked about a Zoom portable podcast recorder. Don't. Those are tabletop mixers built for two to four people sitting still around a desk mic. Your show does not sit still — episode 1 has you holding a phone up to the lens, ep 4 is on the floor with a kid, ep 8 is at the kitchen table and then the recycling bin, ep 6 is on location at an archive. A desk box anchors you to a desk and adds a second device to sync at 7am.

Buy the thing that clips to your shirt and follows you.

SkipZoom PodTrak / AMS series~$150–220
Wrong shape for this show. Built for seated multi-mic podcasts. You'd be paying for three inputs you never use and gaining a sync step you don't want.
If you already own one, fine — use it for scoring the music at your desk, not for the show.
BuyWireless lav, 2-transmitter kit~$90–170
DJI Mic Mini or Rode Wireless Micro. Clips on, pairs straight to the iPhone, and — the part that actually matters — records a safety copy onto the transmitter itself. If the wireless link hiccups, the audio still exists. That single feature is worth the whole purchase.
Get the two-transmitter kit, not the single. Episode 4 is you and the kid; episode 9 is bedtime. You'll want the second mic and you will not want to re-buy in October. Check current price before you order — these move.
BuyOverhead phone arm + clamp~$25–35
The one thing you genuinely cannot improvise. The Palette shot is the show's signature and it is a locked-off top-down of the desk. Any articulating arm that clamps to a desk edge and holds a phone face-down will do. This is the highest-value $30 in the whole season.
Already haveCamera, lights, screen capture$0
Camera: your iPhone. Shoot 4K/30, locked exposure, locked focus. Do not shoot 60 — you don't need slow motion except one shot in ep 3, and 60 doubles your file sizes for nothing.
Light: a window. Face it. That's the whole lighting plan.
Screen: QuickTime screen recording on the Mac, free. Include system audio.

The two-camera problem, and the answer: don't solve it. One phone cannot shoot your face and the overhead at the same time. Do not buy a second camera and do not try to run both.

The overhead is a locked-off insert of a hand placing cards on a desk. Shoot it separately, as its own setup, with no continuity to match. Nobody will ever know, and it saves you an hour and a headache every single week.

03 — THE SIX CARDS

These are the 98%. Write them tonight, in pen.

The whole episode turns on these. The model brings the HTML — it has written ten million dashboards. It has never heard of The Wall, or the novel, or the fact that you have eleven projects and a six-year-old. That comes off the cards.

Keep them short and a little sloppy. You wrote these over coffee, not in a planning session. One line per card, plus whatever scrap of status you actually remember. Two or three lines is a whole card — if you're writing a fourth, you're planning, not remembering.

Read me

Only the typewriter text on the paper gets written on the card. The grey line under each card is my note to you about why it's worded that way — that stays off the card.

CARD 1THE WALL btc art thing — stuck on what?

CARD 2NOVEL Meatbag Made ch. 4?

The question mark is the point.

A number it can actually use.

Teaches it that "done" is a state.

CARD 5KID STUFF roblox + school

Not a project. It won't know that yet.

CARD 6GYM

The joke. One word, nothing else. Write it last. Sets up the happy accident at 4:40.

Card six is load-bearing. The scripted beat — "it thinks gym is a project… which, honestly, fair" — only happens if GYM is sitting there in the pile looking exactly like the other five. Don't flag it, don't explain it, don't write anything else on it. One word.

06 — PERFORMANCE

The register is the whole differentiator.

Ross's trick was never the painting. It was that he talked like someone who had nowhere else to be. That's a performance, and it's the one you have to actually rehearse.

✓
Talk at 80% of your normal volume.The mic is six inches from your mouth. Let it do the work.
✓
Drop your pitch at the end of every sentence.Upspeak reads as asking permission. Ross never once asked permission.
✓
Let three seconds of nothing happen.It will feel like thirty. It reads as confidence.
✓
Say "muy relaxando" exactly once.In the outro. Twice makes it a bit; once makes it yours.
×
Never start a sentence with "So…"It's the tell of someone about to explain. You're not explaining, you're doing.
×
Never apologize for the wait."Sorry, this takes a second" tells the viewer the wait is a defect. It isn't. It's the pacing.
×
Never explain what AI is.Not one sentence, all season. The audience is non-technical, not uninformed.
×
Never mention the other guy.Not in the video, not in the title, not in the description. Ross never named another painter in eleven years.
×
No "hey guys," no "don't forget to subscribe."The cold open is a sentence typing itself. That's the open, forever.
07 — WHEN IT BREAKS

Keep rolling. A real accident beats the scripted one.

The gym beat at 4:40 is a backup, not a requirement. If the model does something genuinely wrong earlier in the take — misreads a card, invents a project you don't have, builds something hideous — use that instead and cut the scripted beat. Real is better and it's not close.

1
Don't stop recording.You cannot get authentic back once you've reset for it.
2
Never argue with it on camera.No "no, that's wrong." Only ever: here's what you didn't know. That rule is the entire show's thesis and episode 7 is built on it.
3
If it's still broken after two tries, fix it by hand and say so."I'm going to do this the boring way" is a perfectly good beat. Hiding it is not.
4
If the whole build collapses — shoot it anyway.Then it's episode 7, a month early, and it's the best thing you'll make all season.

09 — THE FIVE SATURDAYS

Revised, because two episodes on day one isn't real.

The bible says two per Saturday. Day one gets one — you're learning the rig, and that tax is real and only gets paid once. Two continuity facts also force the order:

Episode 5 has to be shot near-last, because at 4:40 you place all four earlier palette objects on the desk. You can't place props you haven't shot yet. Episode 10 gets shot during the release window, not before — it uses real viewer prompts, and those don't exist until episode 1 is public.
